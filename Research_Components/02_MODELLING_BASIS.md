# Modelling basis and thesis plan: asymmetric high-rise damped outriggers

## Research problem and decision
Determine how the elevation of movable outriggers changes the bidirectional nonlinear seismic response of a fixed asymmetric high-rise building, for (O) conventional outriggers, (O+B) outriggers with a belt, and (O+B+C) outriggers with belt and roof cap. Compare ordinary rigid connections, fluid-viscous-damped connections and separately defined BRB alternatives. Evaluate both ULS and SLS hazards. The roof cap stays at the roof: it is not moved with the candidate outrigger. Exact truss topology, connection locations and permitted mechanical-floor elevations must be stated before analysis.

Recommended thesis title: **Performance-based optimization of outrigger elevation and damping in asymmetric reinforced-concrete high-rise buildings under bidirectional earthquake excitation**.

The hypothesis is that location minimizing roof translation need not minimize edge drift, torsion, acceleration or residual deformation; the best location can change with belt/cap configuration, hazard level and device sizing. Investigate this instead of claiming a universal optimum at a prescribed fraction of height. The 2021 study *Optimal outrigger locations and damping parameters for single-outrigger systems considering earthquake and wind excitations*, Engineering Structures245,112868 (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.engstruct.2021.112868), is directly relevant to comparing ordinary, viscous and BRB outriggers. Its simplified-system optimum is a search guide, not the answer for this asymmetric building. *Analysis of Tall Buildings with Damped Outriggers*, JSE136(11),2010 (https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)ST.1943-541X.0000247), supplies the mechanism and location/damping coupling basis.

## Recommended material hierarchy
Use Concrete02 with Steel02 for nonlinear RC frame fibers. Keep Concrete01 as a compression-only model-form comparison, not a second concrete material superimposed on the same fiber. Use ConcreteCM with SteelMPF inside FSAM for wall panels, within **E-SFI-MVLEM-3D**. It is different from the original SFI-MVLEM-3D formulation. The author reports substantially reduced computation and improved convergence for E-SFI in their examples; this does not guarantee a runtime ratio for our building. Prefer it for the study if single-panel and core benchmarks converge and match independent response evidence.

Concrete02 includes tensile softening; Concrete01 has no concrete tensile resistance. ConcreteCM provides a different cyclic concrete law suitable for FSAM. A concrete cube grade does not specify unloading, tension, confinement or crushing-strain parameters. Fe500 specifies nominal yield grade, not all cyclic Steel02 coefficients. We can proceed with an explicit nominal research model and measure sensitivity without mislabelling it experimentally calibrated.

- Concrete01/02: fpc=-24MPa, epsc0=-0.002, residual fpcu=-4.8MPa and epsU=-0.006. The peak strain, residual20% and terminal strain are ASSUMED unconfined backbone choices. They are not a transcription from Mander's specimens. The initial tangent is exactly2*fpc/epsc0=24000MPa. Concrete02 unloading ratio0.10 is also an assumption.
- Concrete02 ft=2MPa is a nominal assumed tensile strength. For an illustrative characteristic length0.30m and assumed postpeak fracture energy Gf=0.10kN/m, the triangular postpeak relation gives Ets=ft^2*lch/(2*Gf)=6,000,000kN/m2=6000MPa. Changing mesh/hinge length requires updating this relation. A length-adjusted parameter is not proof of mesh-objective response. The energy concerns the postpeak stress versus crack-opening idealization; the continuum strain mapping must be consistent.
- ConcreteCM: fpcc=-24MPa, epcc=-0.002, Ec=24000MPa to match the frame initial tangent, rc=7, xcrn=1.035, ft=2MPa, et=ft/Ec=0.0000833333, rt=1.2, xcrp=10000. The shape/critical-strain coefficients are STARTING ASSUMPTIONS requiring envelope/cyclic sensitivity. xcrp10000 represents a long tension-stiffening tail as documented; it is not standalone plain-concrete fracture calibration and is particularly important to test for wall mesh dependence.
- Do not silently use 5000*sqrt(30)=27386MPa in the elastic reference while expecting these24GPa Concrete01/02 cards to reproduce exactly the same periods. Match actual initial tangents, or document the intentional difference. Do not multiply nonlinear fibers by cracked-section factors again without demonstrating the intended effective stiffness; this can double-reduce stiffness.
- No enhanced wall boundary confinement is assigned where the project has no confined boundary details. A wall edge is not automatically a confined boundary. For tied columns, compute confined core response from actual hoops, spacing, longitudinal layout, effective lateral confinement and steel properties. An assumed strength multiplier such as1.2 or1.3 is not a substitute. Cover stays unconfined.

### FVD reference versus building sizing
Use the OpenSees nonlinear Maxwell ViscousDamper law: spring K in series with dashpot F=Cd*|v_dashpot|^alpha*sign(v_dashpot). Dashpot velocity is not generally total assembly velocity. Akcelyan, Lignos and Hikino(2018), *Adaptive Numerical Method Algorithms for Nonlinear Viscous and Bilinear Oil Damper Models Subjected to Dynamic Loading*, Soil Dynamics and Earthquake Engineering113,488-502, plus Akcelyan et al.(2016),JSE142(6),04016024, are the preferred numerical/full-scale evaluation references listed by the developers: https://opensees.berkeley.edu/wiki/index.php?title=ViscousDamper_Material .

The bundled verification card reproduces official example inputs K300kN/mm,Cd280.3kN(s/mm)^0.3,alpha0.30. Metre-unit conversion is K300000kN/m and Cd=280.3*1000^0.30, not Cd*1000. The example is not the appropriate device size merely because it is published.

For a separate hypothetical STUDY budget, choose Fref1000kN per device at vref0.30m/s and alpha0.30; calculate Cd=Fref/vref^alpha. Choose K100000kN/m for initial investigation, equivalent to EA/L with E200GPa,A0.005m2,L10m. These are explicit sizing assumptions. Sweep alpha0.3/0.5/1.0 at the SAME Fref and vref; recalculate Cd so exponents are compared fairly. Hold total device count/capacity budget consistent across positions; alternatively show a cost/capacity Pareto frontier. Obtain force, velocity, stroke, temperature and cumulative-energy limits from a selected device design before making a real-device adequacy claim. Dampers need relative displacement across the intended connection; do not put both ends on DOFs tied equal by the diaphragm.

### BRB and conventional steel trusses
Fe500 is REINFORCEMENT, not automatically the SHS outrigger steel or the BRB core. Retain each actual structural steel grade in the source model. For the hypothetical BRB component card only, Fy355MPa,E200GPa,A0.002m2,Lyield3m give Py710kN and Ky133333.33kN/m. These are assumed design variables, not measured brace properties.

Black, Makris and Aiken(2002), *Component Testing, Stability Analysis and Characterization of Buckling-Restrained Unbonded Braces*, PEER2002/08, https://peer.berkeley.edu/publications/2002-08 , is the selected component-test reference. It validates those tested brace assemblies, not every Steel02+corotTruss implementation. Model yielding-core/non-yielding/end-zone stiffness consistently; test tension/compression overstrength, fatigue and allowable cumulative plastic deformation. Conventional trusses may buckle: a yielding-only axial corotTruss cannot establish their postbuckling performance. Use an appropriate geometric-imperfection and bending-capable brace model, or verify the ordinary members remain within a justified elastic/buckling-safe range and state this limitation.

Do not assume the patch is the entire95m core.

Rigid loading beams in laboratory models can represent test apparatus; they do not require adding fictitious physical beams to your core. Shared corner nodes, actual floor/core connections and wall openings must be represented without adding artificial confinement, equalDOF constraints or extra bending stiffness. Do not apply a separate beam where a wall spandrel already represents its physical concrete. The E-SFI patch only tests a planar setup. Published SD-06 tests are shear-dominated squat nonplanar walls; a slender high-rise core additionally needs slender-wall/flexural, axial-load, corner and mesh checks. Lu's layered-shell route is a useful independent model-form comparison after reproducing its actual specimen, not a drop-in replacement parameter set for an unconfined core. A reference photograph or converged patch is not that validation.

### Transfer rule and current stop conditions

1. Separate each value into (a) measured project property, (b) value transcribed from a specific paper specimen, (c) constitutive-model default, or (d) explicit sensitivity assumption. A literature value is transferable only when its definition, units, material/grade, specimen, and loading regime match; otherwise it is a benchmark input, not a project parameter.
2. Verify units and command order against current OpenSees/OpenSeesPy documentation, then run a material coupon/protocol check, member/component reproduction, and only then building-level validation. Compare curves and declared numerical metrics, not just a successful return code. Keep a held-out test/history for validation when parameters are fitted.
3. Before comparing FVD/BRB outriggers, add and verify per-device force, relative displacement/stroke, velocity, and cumulative energy outputs. Keep device self-weight/mass, connections, geometry, and damping accounting consistent across baseline and device cases.
4. Do not rank any configuration from incomplete or failed histories. The saved building diagnostic shows the configured gravity preload converges, but the Kobe excitation fails at the first transient step with return code `-3`; the cause is unresolved. The current Python environment also lacks OpenSeesPy, so no new native solver result has been produced in this review.

## Structural and numerical decisions that affect the optimum
- Use true3D mass distribution and an actual diaphragm master at each floor mass centre; retain eccentric core/columns. Report maximum edge drifts at multiple perimeter points, not only master-node translation. Direction swaps preserve paired bidirectional input and signs. Investigate accidental torsion separately from inherent asymmetry with the adopted code procedure.
- Rigid diaphragms constrain in-plane kinematics; they are not vertical load-bearing slabs. Use compatible slab bending/load transfer or a documented tributary transfer with no double-counted floor/partition/selfweight. Gravity reactions matching an incomplete applied load inventory does not prove all loads were imported.
- RC force-based fibers can develop axial extension as the neutral axis shifts. A rigid in-plane diaphragm may restrain beam end extension and introduce axial force; fiber beams are not universally prohibited. Check the physical restraint against a semi-rigid slab/diaphragm sensitivity model and inspect beam axial demand. Do not remove legitimate outrigger axial action merely to eliminate the symptom. Source: OpenSees diaphragm modelling guidance https://opensees.berkeley.edu/wiki/index.php/Modeling_Diaphragms_in_2D_Models_with_Linear_and_Nonlinear_Elements%2C_Oct_2011 .
- Include gravity/P-Delta before excitation. Keep structural hysteresis and FVD dissipation separate from background viscous damping. Choose2% background damping as a RESEARCH baseline with1%/3% sensitivities, not as an NBC prescribed value. Verify every inspected mode against the code damping restriction and use a documented nonlinear damping implementation; do not use a large stiffness-proportional term to hide convergence problems.
- Begin Newmark gamma0.5,beta0.25 and converged Newton iterations with recorded tolerances. Compare dt and dt/2 on the same model/input and compare peaks/energy, not only returncode0. Enforce the code time-step rule using the actual translational/effective modal periods and record digitization. A completed0.05s patch or1s beginning of a long earthquake is not a full record result.
- Use3 versus5 versus7 integration points/appropriate hinge regularization to test result sensitivity, not just performance. Avoid changing material, mesh and solver simultaneously. Archive model hash, material card hash, record units, scale and solver settings per case.

## NBC 105:2025 and ULS/SLS interpretation
Use the official code https://ebps.kathmandu.gov.np/UploadFiles/NBC_105_2025_Sesmic_Design.pdf . Sections9.3.1-9.3.3 govern nonlinear modelling, gravity/P-Delta/torsion, records and response evaluation. Distinguish seismic-weight/preload gravity from strength load combinations. Section5.5.3 limits interstory drift to0.025ULS and0.006SLS. Use each hazard's intended spectrum and the chapter9 scaling rules; never apply the ULS ESM reduction Rmu again to nonlinear forces or multiply directly integrated nonlinear displacements by Rmu. Save raw and design-check demands. ULS/SLS are separate hazard verifications of one physical building; do not automatically change actual reinforcement or constitutive strengths between them. Linear effective stiffness cases and nonlinear material state are different concepts.

The publisher's PDF was discoverable but full retrieval timed out in this review; do not interpret this memo as clause-by-clause certification of existing code. Check the selected code edition's spectral target, scaling band/factors, direction swaps, step bound, damping and record aggregation in a signed run audit before design conclusions. Use8independent orthogonal pairs as the STUDY suite, bothXY/YX; this also avoids relying on an ambiguous exactly7-record interpretation. Code drift compliance is not automatically Operational/IO/LS/CP: component deformation and failure limits require the applicable standard, detailing and failure mode; no universal fiber strain/rotation thresholds are invented here.
